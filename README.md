# 👥 Thinking and Feeling: Turing Meets Angelou
## 🎯 Objective <br>
This project brings together machine thinking and human feeling through Gamma presentations inspired by Alan Turing’s “Computing Machinery and Intelligence” and Maya Angelou’s “When I Think About Myself.” Both presentations use prompts grounded in shared standards with adjustments suited to each source. Together, they explore how AI-assisted visual storytelling can communicate scientific arguments and poetic emotion. <p>

## ⌨️ Presentation Prompts
### 🤵🏻 Alan Turing Presentation Prompt
<details>
<summary><strong>View the Turing Presentation Prompt</strong></summary><p></p>
Create a 10-slide presentation explaining the main ideas in Alan Turing’s “Computing Machinery and Intelligence” for people who have not read the paper. Give viewers a clear understanding of his central question, key arguments, and conclusions.

Use the provided paper as the primary source. Build a clear narrative from Turing’s opening question to the imitation game, his key arguments and responses to objections, and his discussion of learning machines. Define unfamiliar terms in plain language without oversimplifying his ideas. End with a concise takeaway that accurately reflects the paper.

Ensure every claim accurately represents the paper. Clearly identify direct quotations, and distinguish Turing’s predictions, proposals, and hypothetical examples from established facts. Do not invent data or attribute views to Turing that the paper does not support. Include page or section references in speaker notes.

Use charts only when numerical information in the paper supports a meaningful visual comparison. Clearly label values and explain whether they represent predictions, examples, or observations. When a chart is unsuitable, use a simple conceptual diagram that helps explain an idea.

Follow UX design principles with simple layouts, clear visual hierarchy, readable typography, strong contrast, and consistent formatting. Give each slide one main idea and a descriptive title that communicates its takeaway. Keep visible text to approximately 20–30 words per slide, including the title but excluding brief source labels, so it can be read in about 10 seconds. Place supporting explanations and citations in speaker notes.

Reflect the logical, inquisitive, and precise nature of Turing’s writing. Use artwork from 1912–1954 or visuals inspired by artistic styles from that period, with particular emphasis on the paper’s 1950 context. Ensure visuals support the content, avoid anachronisms, and do not imply that Turing endorsed a particular artistic style.

</details>

### 💃🏾 Maya Angelou Presentation Prompt
<details>
<summary><strong>View the Angelou Presentation Prompt</strong></summary><p></p>

Create a 1-slide presentation explaining the main ideas in Maya Angelou’s “When I Think About Myself” for people who have not read the poem. Give viewers a clear understanding of its central themes, human experiences, and emotions.

Use the provided poem as the primary source. Build a clear narrative from the speaker’s opening reflections to her experiences and feelings, and her use of laughter in response to pain. Define unfamiliar terms in plain language without oversimplifying her ideas. End with a concise takeaway that accurately reflects the poem.

Ensure every claim accurately represents the poem. Clearly identify direct quotations, and distinguish interpretations supported by the text from established facts about Angelou’s life and intentions. Do not invent details or attribute views to Angelou that the poem does not support. Include line or stanza references in speaker notes.

Use visuals only when imagery, symbolism, or relationships in the poem support a meaningful visual interpretation. Clearly connect visuals to the text and distinguish literal descriptions from interpretive representations. When a conceptual diagram is unsuitable, use a simple image that helps explain an idea or emotion.

Follow UX design principles with simple layouts, clear visual hierarchy, readable typography, strong contrast, and consistent formatting. Give each slide one main idea and a descriptive title that communicates its takeaway. Keep visible text to approximately 20–30 words per slide, including the title but excluding brief source labels, so it can be read in about 10 seconds. Place supporting explanations and citations in speaker notes.

Reflect the tone, perspective, and expressive qualities of Angelou’s poem. Use artwork from 1928–2014 or visuals inspired by artistic styles from that period, with particular emphasis on the poem’s 1971 context. Ensure visuals support the content, avoid anachronisms, and do not imply that Angelou endorsed a particular artistic style.

</details>
